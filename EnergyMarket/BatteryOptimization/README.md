# Battery Energy Storage Optimization

An optimization model that maximizes the profit of a utility-scale battery operating in the PJM wholesale electricity market through energy arbitrage.
The model considers a 100 MW / 400 MWh battery that earns revenue by charging when wholesale electricity prices are low and discharging when prices are high.

The optimal schedule is not trivial as prices fluctuate across 24 hours and every charging decision depletes capacity needed for future discharge.
Since the profit has a linear dependency on the schedule ($(discharge - charge)\cdot price$) the model relies on linear programming to find the optimal schedule.


# Price Data

- [EIA Wholesale Markets CSV](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/wholesalemarkets/data.php?rto=pjm) (no API key required).
- Data correspond to the Allegheny Power System (APS) zone (Pennsylvania, Virginia, West Virginia, and Maryland) within the PJM Interconnection grid.
- Raw Data Features [only the useful ones]:  "UTC Timestamp (Interval Ending)", "Allegheny Power System LMP"


# Optimization Problem

**Decision variables** (per hour $t$):
- $c_t$: charging power (MW)
- $d_t$: discharging power (MW)
- $s_t$: state of charge (SOC) (MWh)

**Objective:** maximise daily operating profit
$$P = \max \sum_{t=1}^{24} \lambda_t \cdot (d_t - c_t) \cdot \Delta t$$


**Constraints:**
- Power limits: $c_t, d_t \leq 100$ MW
- SOC bounds: $0 \leq s_t \leq 400$ MWh
- SOC dynamics: $s_t = s_{t-1} + \eta_c c_t - \frac{1}{\eta_d} d_t$

**Other:**
- Efficiency: $\eta_c = \eta_d = \sqrt{RTE} \approx 0.949$


# Simulation Methodology

* Single-Day Optimization:
We solve the 24-hour LP for a representative high-spread day to illustrate the optimal dispatch strategy.
* Full simulation:
The LP is solved independently for each calendar day in the dataset.
Each day begins at 50% SOC (200 MWh).
50% is arbitrarily chosen as a neutral starting point that avoids biasing the optimizer toward charging or discharging in the first hours.
Days are treated as independent (no SOC carryover between days).
Daily profits are aggregated and scaled to estimate annualized revenue. 
* Sensitivity Analysis
We vary one parameter at a time to quantify its impact on annualized revenue.

# Potential Enhancements

- **Ancillary services co-optimization**:
Optimize battery participation in both energy arbitrage and grid support services such as frequency regulation, spinning reserve, or capacity markets. The model decides how much battery capacity to allocate to each revenue stream while respecting operational constraints.
- **Stochastic programming**:
Explicitly model uncertainty in future electricity prices using multiple price scenarios with associated probabilities. The optimizer finds decisions that perform well across all scenarios, producing more robust operating strategies than deterministic optimization.
- **Multi-day lookahead** (Rolling Model Predictive Control - MPC):
Instead of optimizing battery operation for only the next 24 hours, optimize over a longer horizon (e.g., 3–7 days). At each day or hour, re-run the optimization with updated data while implementing only the first decision step. This allows the battery to anticipate future high-price periods and avoid shortsighted decisions.
- **Price forecasting integration**:
Current model assumes perfect foresight. It knows the elctricity prices for the entire optimization horizon. Replace actual market prices (perfect foresight) with predictions generated by a forecasting model (e.g., ARIMA, LSTM, XGBoost, Prophet). This is how it's done in real-time markets. Operators use price forecasts and repeatedly re-optimize as new information arrives. The optimization then operates under realistic information, allowing evaluation of how forecast accuracy impacts profitability.
